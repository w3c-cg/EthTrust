Goals

The core goal of the group is to maintain the EthTrust specification, and provide additional materials to support adoption and implementation.

Deliverables

Specifications

The EthTrust Specification based on an agreed Editor's Draft, as version 4 or subsequent versions.

The group MAY decide to generalise the specification to cover other programming languages, or other blockchains.

The group will not publish Specifications on other topics.

Non-Normative Reports

The group may produce other Community Group Reports within the scope of this charter, for instance examples and case studies, implementation reports, or white papers.

Test Suites and Other Software

The group should produce test suites to support the Specification licensed under the .

Dependencies or Liaisons

The Group's work has significant dependencies on the Solidity Compiler, and its documentation.

Community and Business Group Process

The group operates under the Community and Business Group Process. Terms in this Charter that conflict with those of the Community and Business Group Process are void.

To protect the ability of the community at large to innovate and implement standards, and to ensure the work of the Community Group can be widely used, W3C seeks organizational licensing commitments from individuals' employer(s) or projects in which the individual has a significant involvement, under the W3C Community Contributor License Agreement (CLA).

When people request to participate without representing their organization's legal interests, W3C generally approves requests for this group on the understanding that they will seek and expect an organizational commitment from an individual's employer, if the individual makes a contribution to a group Deliverable. The section on Contribution Mechanics describes how W3C expects to monitor these contribution requests.

The W3C Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct applies to participation in this group.

Contribution Mechanics

Substantive Contributions to Specifications can only be made by Community Group Participants who have agreed to the W3C Community Contributor License Agreement (CLA).

Specifications and Test Suites created in the Community Group must use the W3C Software and Document License. All other documents produced by the group should use that License where possible.

Community Group participants agree that all contributions will be recorded in the EthTrust GitHub repository. The preferred mechanism for feedback is a pull request (preferred), by raising an issue, or by adding a comment to an existing issue.

All Github repositories attached to the Community Group must contain a copy of the W3C Community group CONTRIBUTING and LICENSE files.

Transparency

The group will conduct all of its technical work in public, ensuring technical contributions are recorded in its GitHub repositories.

Meetings should not but may be restricted to Community Group participants.

A public summary or minutes of each meeting must be posted to the group's GitHub repository.

Decision Process

This group will seek to make decisions where there is consensus.

Decisions are made on proposals posted as part of the agenda of a meeting provided at least 48 hours in advance, taking into account feedback provided to the group in GitHub Issues or through the mailing list, with consensus assessed by the chair of the Group.

Decisions of the group must be noted in minutes of a meeting.

If substantial disagreement remains (e.g. the group is divided) and the group needs to decide a technical issue in order to continue to make progress, the chair will choose an alternative that has substantial support, typically by conducting a vote. Individuals who disagree with the choice are encouraged to maintain an alternative fork, with a goal of gaining implementation experience, that can inform revisiting the Group's resolution of the issue.

It is the Chairs' responsibility to ensure that the decision process is fair, respects the consensus of the CG, and does not unreasonably favour or discriminate against any group participant or their employer.

Changing Chair(s)

Participants in this group choose can replace the Chair, if 5 participants, no two from the same organisation, call for an election in email to the Group's internal mailing list (archive).

If this happens, or the chair announces they are standing down, or is absent and unresponsive for 6 weeks, the following process applies:

  1. The Chair, the W3C Community Development Lead, or if the conditions are met for an election but it has not been announced within 7 days any participant, announces the start of an election by email to the Group's internal mailing list (archive).
  2. Participants announce themselves as a candidate using the Group's internal mailing list (archive), within the shorter of 14 days or whenever all participants have stated they are or are not standing as a candidate.
  3. If there are no candidates announced within 14 days, then within a further 21 days any participant can restart the election process. Otherwise, W3C should close the group.
  4. If there is only one candidate, that person becomes the Chair.
  5. If there are two or more candidates, the individual who announced the beginning of the election will create the election, on a platform accessbile to all participants, that implements Meek or Schulze-based ranked choice voting, and announce it by email to the Group's internal mailing list (archive). (If this does not happen within 7 days, any participant can create and announce the election on a platform of their choice).
  6. Participants have the shorter of 21 days or until all participants have voted, to vote.
  7. The Chair may appoint co-Chairs.

Participants dissatisfied with the outcome of an election may ask the W3C Community Development Lead to intervene. The Community Development Lead, after evaluating the election, may take any action including no action.

Amending this Charter

The group may decide to work on a proposed amended charter, editing the text using the Decision Process described above.

The group may make simple corrections that make no substantive changes to the goals, scope, deliverables, decision process or rules for amending the charter using its normal decision process.

The decision on whether to adopt an amended charter that makes substantive changes to the goals, scope, deliverables, decision process or rules for amending the charter must allow a 30-day vote on the proposed new charter.

A new charter that makes substantive changes to the goals, scope, deliverables, decision process or rules for amending the charter must receive at least 2/3 of the votes cast and from a majority of organisations whose representatives cast a vote, to pass.

The new charter, if approved, takes effect 7 days after consensus, or if the group is unable to reach consensus result of a vote, is announced.
